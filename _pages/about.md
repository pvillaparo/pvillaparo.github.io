I am an Economics PhD student at the University of Michigan, working across public finance, labor, industrial organization, and environmental and energy economics. Studying bundled reforms under labor market power showed me how taxes, mandates, and regulation shape not only what firms do, but also wages, employment, and who gets the rents. I am also interested in how public finance reaches energy markets and local economies, including how changing the incentives to be formal can affect pollution and deforestation.

For me, these questions started at home, in Cusco, in the Peruvian Andes. My family's history was shaped by large reforms in land and education that opened opportunities for vulnerable families to thrive, and that is why I care about labor markets, public policy, and the institutions that decide who can access opportunity. And growing up near regions rich in minerals and natural gas, yet surrounded by extraordinary biodiversity, pushed me to think about the tradeoffs between development, energy, and environmental protection.

My path into research also began in Peru, where I earned a BA in Economics at the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú (PUCP) and worked on laboratory experiments at GECE-PUCP. Along the way, I managed field experiments with Innovations for Poverty Action (IPA) and was a research assistant at the Universidad del Pacífico Research Center. Before my PhD, I was an Economics Research Specialist at Princeton University, and from there I came to Michigan with the questions that started at home.
