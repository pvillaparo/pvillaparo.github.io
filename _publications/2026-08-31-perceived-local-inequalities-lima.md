This paper studies how perceptions of own social class and local affluence shape preferences for redistribution. Using a survey experiment in Lima Metropolitana, we show that corrective information can affect redistributive attitudes when perceived-actual gaps are large, and that these effects depend on the type and magnitude of misperceptions.
